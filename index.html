Section 2 · Climate-Related Risks and Challenges

The industry is going the wrong way

2020 = 100. Sources: Leal Filho et al. (2022); H&M Foundation (2026).

Making clothes causes 8–10% of the world's greenhouse gases — more than all planes and ships put together.

And it is concentrated: dyeing and finishing alone is about 55% of it — that runs on heat from coal and gas boilers, not electricity.

Three kinds of danger

1 · rules tightening  (transition risk)

SEBI, California's SB 253/261 and the EU due-diligence directive force reporting on firms and their suppliers. Fall behind and buyers drop you.

2 · water and weather  (physical risk)

Dyeing causes up to 20% of industrial water pollution — and needs clean water to run. Drought squeezes both ends (IPCC, 2023).

3 · losing trust  (reputational risk)

Greenwashing and labour claims are now testable under frameworks like the CSRD. Getting caught costs contracts.

📈All three land in the same place: costs up, orders lost, dye-house assets written off early — climate risk as a question of long-term profitability.

Section 4 · Climate Adaptation and Mitigation Strategies

Two jobs: stop it getting worse, survive what's here

Mitigation — stop making it worse
Cleaner electricity. Rooftop solar, group-captive wind, open-access PPAs. The easiest lever.
Cleaner heat — the big one. Crop-waste boilers, heat pumps, heat recovery. This is the 55%.
Waste less. Low-liquor dyeing, cold pad-batch, right-first-time colour. Cheapest per tonne.
Better cloth. Recycled polyester and cotton, recovered cutting waste.
Adaptation — survive what's already here
Water. Tirupur dye houses already run zero liquid discharge, by court order. A drier baseline is the new test.
People. 6 lakh workers in sheds near the limit of safe continuous work. Insulation, ventilation, rest protocols.
Buildings and suppliers. Flood-proofing, raised electricals, insurance. Backup processors qualified before trouble hits.
⚡You cannot fix steam with a solar panel. And the boiler that causes the 55% takes 5 to 7 years to repay, against loans that run for months. Swastik, over to you.

The pollution is here. The money goes there.

Why small firms can't borrow · 1

Too slow to repay. The boiler takes 5–7 years; ordinary loans run for months.

2

Nothing is measured. You cannot be rewarded for saving water nobody counted.

Green isn't much cheaper. The real prize is discipline and new investors.

Section 5 · Ethical and Climate Governance Issues
We are sustainable, but not completely. We are trying to cope up sustainability standards to the maximum which we can.
— Ritwik Duresami, Director of Operations, Digsel · our own interview, Tirupur

That candour is the opposite of greenwashing — he called compliance a responsibility towards the communities where we operate, not just a requirement. But intent is not governance: in a listed firm the board owns climate risk and SEBI's rules make it auditable, while a twelve-machine unit has no board at all.

Who pays
Banks and big buyers — they price credit on proof
Who checks
An independent auditor. SEBI's BRSR makes it compulsory for big listed firms and their suppliers
Who collects
The town's trade association — one shared template, shared cost
The factory floor
Each unit records electricity, steam, water, waste, working conditions
Real, or just good marketing? · click to flip
30% recycled polyester, checked by an outside agency, and we publish our factory list.
realSpecific number, independent check, traceable.
Carbon neutral by 2050, using high-quality offsets.
greenwashingBeyond anyone's career, no interim target, offsets instead of cuts.
Our dye house recycles 100% of its water, certified by the state board.
true, not an achievementIn Tirupur this is the law. Compliance dressed as effort.
🧩Take out the “who collects” layer and it collapses: one small factory can't produce proof alone, so the bank reverts to demanding property. That is where our main recommendation comes from.

Section 7 · Six recommendations, and who has to act
01 · banks · the headlineFund MSMEs as a cluster, with tenors matched to the 5–7 year payback.
02 · governmentMake the schemes findable, with a “transition” category so crop-waste boilers qualify.
03 · big buyersFund suppliers, don't just audit them. Inspecting a factory doesn't buy it a boiler.
04 · factoriesMeasure first, borrow second — with systems built for a 12-machine unit.
05 · associationsCollect the data and buy the checking together, so greenwashing has nowhere to hide.
06 · everyone · the conditionA just transition — no MSME, worker or supplier left behind.
🔗Why five years? Because the boiler takes 5 to 7 years to repay. And no actor appears twice — which is why the answer is a shared structure. The pollution is in the dye house; the money isn't. Build the bridge. Thank you — questions?
